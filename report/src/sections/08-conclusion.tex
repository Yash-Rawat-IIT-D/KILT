\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

KILT connects LTL trace semantics to an executable model checker through
formula compilation, automata products and finite graph search. The
correctness results establish that the Boolean verdict agrees with universal
satisfaction over initial infinite executions. Counterexample extraction adds
a further guarantee: the particular returned lasso describes a valid initial
execution that violates the supplied property. These results compose contracts
for normalisation, tableau acceptance, generalised B\"uchi conversion and graph
search into a public checking interface.

The guarantees apply to the declared finite Kripke model, with nonempty
initial states and an explicit seriality proof; they impose no fairness
assumption. The worked examples illustrate safety violations, indefinite
waiting and the difference between reachability and guaranteed progress.
The performance evaluation covers small models, and larger stress tests
remain necessary to assess practical scalability. Choosing a model and
formula that describe the intended system remains part of the specification
work.

The project also made the engineering role of definitions and theorem
interfaces concrete. Separating the automata, tableau and graph components
helped preserve their contracts through implementation changes and supported
the later addition of lasso extraction and lightweight proof automation.
The next steps are community review, clearer module organisation and
documentation, larger evaluations, and proposed CSLib contributions from
the reusable components. CTL* remains a longer-term extension beyond the
current LTL toolkit.
