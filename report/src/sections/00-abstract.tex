\addsec{Abstract}\label{sec:abstract}

I would like to thank my supervisor, my friends and my family for their
support throughout this project.

I chose KILT as a natural extension of my previous introductory coursework
in Automated Verification. I wanted to explore the mathematical
specifications behind graphs and automata while using Lean to build and
verify a substantial program. I saw the project as an opportunity to bring
together ideas from verification with the practical work of implementing a
model checker.

Through KILT, I developed an LTL toolkit connecting infinite-trace semantics,
automata construction, finite graph search and counterexample extraction.
I established the correspondence between the checker's verdict and the
declared semantics for finite serial Kripke structures, together with
guarantees about the violating executions represented by returned lassos.

I expected a good learning experience, and I found one. Working in Lean
required more planning and trial and error than I was used to in C++ or
Python. I also came away with lessons that extend beyond the language such as
choosing definitions carefully, stating contracts clearly, and preserving
their meaning as the implementation changes. I learned to ask not only whether
an algorithm meets its formal specification but also whether that specification
captures the behaviour I intended to describe.

I now see tools such as Lean as increasingly valuable as more of the effort
in programming shifts towards checking that code agrees with the intended
requirements. I have tried to carry that care into this report by separating
what I have proved, what I have tested and what I believe. I would like to
close with Richard Feynman's reminder from his 1974 Caltech commencement
address:

\vspace{1.5em}

\begin{quote}
  \itshape
  ``The first principle is that you must not fool yourself---and you are the
  easiest person to fool.''
\end{quote}
\hfill Richard P.~Feynman, \emph{Cargo Cult Science} (1974).\footnote{%
